The number you bring is not the number that survives

Add-backs get argued in diligence, often from memory. The owner remembers a one-time legal bill, the controller remembers a personal expense, and the evidence sits in three inboxes and a shared drive. The number a seller walks in with is rarely the number that survives the review.

It is harder when the business runs on several QuickBooks Online or Xero files. Each entity has its own chart of accounts, its own late entries, and its own version of what counts as operating. Someone stitches the EBITDA together by hand, and nobody can say exactly what moved since last month's version.

Example, not a client figure: a seller claims $2.0M of EBITDA and diligence cuts it by 15%. At a 7x multiple, that is about $2.1M of price.

How it works

1

Connect each company

Connect every QuickBooks Online and Xero company. Each profit and loss account gets an EBITDA class, such as revenue, operating expense, interest, taxes, depreciation, or amortization, suggested from its section and name. You can override any of them.

2

Log add-backs with evidence

Addbacks and pro forma adjustments live outside your books. Each one carries a category, a rationale, and evidence. Anyone on the team can draft one, and the team owner approves it.

3

Lock the pack, show what changed

Lock the pack you delivered for a period. The Locked packs view then shows which accounts and adjustments changed its numbers since. Nothing moves without a reason on the page.

What the advisor gets

  • Reported and adjusted EBITDA every month, for each company and for groups of companies you define, kept current from your books in near real-time
  • An add-back register with a category, rationale, evidence, and an owner approval on every item
  • Locked monthly packs, with a list of what changed since each one
  • An Excel or Google Sheets bridge from net income to adjusted EBITDA, monthly and for the trailing twelve months, tied out against the figure in your account
  • A diligence workbook with a cover, monthly profit and loss by entity, the EBITDA bridge, the adjustment schedule, class mapping, changes since the last locked pack, and covenant status. It is a starting point for a quality of earnings review, not a substitute for one
  • Covenant tracking for minimum adjusted EBITDA, total leverage, and fixed charge coverage, with email alerts when one moves into warning or breach

Good to know. Consolidated figures add up entities without intercompany eliminations. Covenant definitions are entered by you and should be checked against your credit agreement.
