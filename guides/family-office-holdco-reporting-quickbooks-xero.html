Your entities multiplied. Your reporting stack didn't.

Family offices and holdcos run 10 to 50 or more entities on QuickBooks and Xero. Keep the family book or group pack live in Excel, custom, not canned.

Family offices and holdcos are multi-entity by default: operating companies, properties, funds, trusts, and personal entities, often each on its own QuickBooks Online or Xero file.

The monthly job is not "run a P&L." It is a consolidated family or group picture in a format no SaaS template ships out of the box. So the work lands on an analyst: export every file, stitch the rollup, eliminate what someone remembered to code as intercompany, and deliver a pack that is already aging when it hits the inbox.

Why entity density breaks the reporting stack

Each new entity is another file to export, another chart of accounts to reconcile against the family layout, and another place an intercompany line or a late adjustment can quietly throw the group number off. The pack is only as current as the last full rebuild, so the analyst spends the first week of every month reassembling last month instead of analysing it. The ledgers are fine. The disconnect between the ledgers and the workbook is the problem.

The wedge is custom, not canned

Canned reporting tools force the family or holdco into their templates. A full ERP migration is a year and six figures you may not need if QuickBooks Online and Xero are still the right ledgers.

Genius Sheets is the middle path: their exact workbook, permanently connected to every entity's books, refreshed daily, with drill-down. Partner capital schedules, management-fee calculations, property rollups, lender packages: the workflows the ledger will never run natively.

Keep the ledger you have. Keep the workbook you built. Connect them, and let the consolidated pack recalculate when the books move.

Custom vs canned, honestly

Template tools are genuinely good at fast, standardised dashboards for a single company. Where they struggle is a bespoke family or group format with partner schedules, fee waterfalls, and lender-specific packages that have to look a particular way. If your reporting has to match a format that already exists, the value is in preserving that format and making it live, not in re-teaching the family to read a new one.

Who this is for

  • Family offices and multi-family offices still on QuickBooks Online or Xero
  • Holdcos and roll-ups that add entities faster than the close process adapts
  • Fractional CFO firms serving those clients: one relationship, many entities

If you are already on NetSuite or Sage Intacct with native consolidation, you are usually a different buyer. If you are living in Excel on top of many QuickBooks Online or Xero files, talk to us.
